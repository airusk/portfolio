The first two weeks:
I started with an action/verb/mechanic that I drew from fun I come across in my own life; skipping rocks; then I wanted to gamify it and have players teleport to where they landed the stone.

My initial philosophy on designing this prototype was to create levels that stretched our player thin unless they skipped rocks for maximal distance when teleporting.

Spoke to codex about my idea in more detail with my professor’s agent given context.
“Pull the conversation from PBOH in my chat log”
This helped to tease out some inner narrative/premise that I hadn’t coaxed out as well as helped me imagine how the mechanic might work in more detail.

I worked through part of the outline the PBOH provided really doing the iterative process until I tuned that mechanic to how I wanted it to interact with the game world.

Created a prototype, received feedback and critique such as:
The game overall felt very zen.
The controls felt intuitive
Explanation for the rules could be better, especially regarding charging a throw.
People didn’t know what they were clearing (stone pathways)

One classmate shared a piece of critique:
“I thought platforms would appear where the stone makes skips”
 Which led me to think of the takeaway that:
It would make sense to ingrain skipping stones more to encourage players to skip stones in the first place.
